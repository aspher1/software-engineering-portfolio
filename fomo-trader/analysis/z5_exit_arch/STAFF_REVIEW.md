# E3 staff review: strongest objections

Built a hermetic, stdlib-only harness (`e3.py`): a fail-closed Z4 quote-path loader, a tick-by-tick simulator for the control ladder and three new architectures behind one common safety floor, E1-mirrored USD cost accounting, a seeded chronological IS/OOS split, E1-style KILL/STOP_ALL replay, the 60% retention and 3× cost gates, and the pre-registered verdicts. It was proven on synthetic paths only and ran on no real data; today's verdict is `awaiting data`. It neither imports nor changes the bot.

## Three strongest objections

1. **A mark-implied bound may buy almost nothing.** The exits these architectures add are small, early partials: +10% or +20% rungs and 10% pullbacks. They fire exactly where thin memecoin pools are least forgiving. E3 already charges 500 bps exit slippage and a 2,000,000-lamport priority fee per partial. At $150 SOL that fee is about $0.30 per transaction, or 3% of a $10 slice. Still, the real cost of a sell is not a constant. It is set by depth at that moment, by latency (a 2-second poll plus the quote round-trip), and by failed sells. None of these appear in a mark. A positive bound tells us where to look, not what we would earn. A negative bound is more informative: if an architecture cannot beat the control on marks after costs, fills will not rescue it. Answer: the honesty clause makes PASS_MARK_BOUND a license only for a fill-feasibility study, never for wiring. This objection remains unanswered until size-aware sell quotes are captured at simulated exit times.

2. **Three architectures frozen today may be anchored to today's regime.** The parameters were chosen now: arm at +20% because the Y3 median peak is +26%, a 20-minute time box, and a σ cutoff of 0.05/√min set without data. They lean on a single Y3 journal snapshot from one bot configuration. By the time 30 paths accumulate, the entry filter, signal mix, or memecoin volatility may have shifted. The σ cutoff is especially exposed: if cached marks flatten returns, every position lands in one regime and variant (c) collapses to a single schedule. Answer: freezing is deliberate. The alternative, tuning after the data arrives, is the forking-paths failure of the 467-config sweep. The run reports `degenerate_regime_split`, and a regime shift shows up as IS/OOS retention failure rather than being hidden. This objection is partly answered: a regime change forces a new, separately pre-registered E3b, not a re-tune of E3.

3. **Thirty trades is thin for three variants plus a control.** At the trigger, IS is 20 and OOS is 10. Picking the best of three on 20 IS closes is a multiple comparison. With per-close P&L dispersion of a few dollars on $10, the IS winner can lead by pure noise. Ten OOS closes cannot confirm it. The decision-capable bar of ≥30 OOS closes implies roughly 90 qualifying positions. At the recent pace, with capture not yet enabled, that is weeks away. Answer: the first run is explicitly `RUNNABLE_NOT_DECISION_CAPABLE` and ships nothing. Selection requires beating the control on the same positions, plus positive IS net-per-close, ≤50% censoring, 60% retention and 3× stress. The dollar KILL/STOP_ALL rules bound the downside of watching. This objection remains unanswered: no multiple-comparison correction is applied, so a PASS must be read as one frozen hypothesis surviving, not as a significance claim.

Additional limitation: **censoring biases E3 toward the control.** A quote path stops when the real bot closed the position. Any variant that would have held longer is marked out at the control's final mark (`path_end`). E3 can therefore measure an architecture's ability to exit *earlier or better* than the ladder, but not the upside it would capture by holding longer. The 50% IS censored-share eligibility rule keeps the worst cases out of selection; the bias itself cannot be removed without paths that outlive the position.
