# Z4 staff review: strongest objections

1. **Long-lived disk growth.** At the configured 2-second sell poll, one continuously open position would generate 43,200 ticks/day. The 5,000-row cap stops that position after 10,000 seconds (2.78 hours). The separate 1 MiB cap can stop it sooner; each position therefore costs at most 1 MiB, and usually roughly 0.5 MiB at about 100 bytes/row. With three slots constantly turning over at the fastest 2.78-hour cap interval, about 26 new positions/day could cost up to 26 MiB/day, or about 0.78 GiB in 30 days. Turnover faster than 2.78 hours can create more files, though each contains fewer rows. There is no global directory cap or automatic deletion. `failed_quotes.jsonl` is also unbounded. Rotation and retention need an operating policy before enabling this for a long run.

2. **Quote paths are stale relative to executable exits.** The row records the manage loop's observed mark, including any normal upstream cache or fallback effects. It is not a size-aware executable sell quote or a transaction fill. Slippage, failed sells, latency, and suddenly vanishing depth can dominate the difference. The observed −92.6% rug gap is a fill problem; a smooth mark path cannot explain it away. The null liquidity field deliberately avoids suggesting that the manage tick saw a depth estimate.

3. **W3 is only partly unblocked.** The path can estimate mark-to-market at candidate timeouts, show when a stale position's observed price first crossed a threshold, and measure how often marks subsequently recovered. The journal still has no intratrade fill prices for counterfactual exits. Z4 cannot validate realized P&L, fill probability, or a safe live timeout. That requires contemporaneous size-aware sell quotes, execution attempts/results, and ideally a prospective paper or small controlled execution test with latency and slippage recorded.
