# W2 coordinated ownership screen (research only)

MELT reports that coordinated accounts hold about 36.5% of launch supply on average and that high-risk tokens' first ten buyers hold roughly 17 percentage points more. This screen explores whether entry-time concentration evidence predicts losses in this journal. These are research observations, not a fitted trading rule.

`detect(snapshot, rule=None)` is pure stdlib and does no I/O except a `W2 FALLBACK` warning when required evidence is missing or malformed. It accepts Solana snapshots with percentage-point fields `holder_top1_pct`, `holder_top5_pct`, and, if ever captured at entry, `first10_buyers_pct` and `launch_block_buyers_pct`. Top-five minus top-one is a crude distribution proxy; a large spread with a modest top-one can flag concentration among several wallets, but cannot establish that those wallets coordinate. The score is the capped maximum of fixed, unfitted feature scales. It is not a probability. The returned `rule` is the exact expression that fired, or `null`. No rule is active by default.

Null paths: missing/non-numeric/non-finite/out-of-range percentages become `None`; `top5 < top1` invalidates top-five and spread while leaving top-one usable. A rule requiring an absent feature approves with one `W2 FALLBACK` warning. An entirely empty or malformed snapshot also approves and logs. BSC always approves and logs. A missing optional feature does not block a rule that needs only an available feature. The wash-score stub always returns `None`: the journal has aggregate buy/sell counts, not per-wallet matched identical-amount transactions or net positions. It never triggers a veto. Missing data fails open because this is an enhancement screen; the existing rug and honeypot guard remains the primary safety check. Any future caller must preserve that guard and forward warnings to its logs.

`python3 -m analysis.w2_bundle_detect.calibrate` regenerates the current inconclusive `verdict.json` using `analysis.replay` fee and slippage conventions. It pairs closed Solana entries, uses journal close append order for chronological first-two-thirds IS / last-third OOS, and stresses the *slippage leg* at 3×. It refuses to write a new verdict once a candidate has enough evidence; full threshold replay must be added then. A candidate needs at least 60 completed Solana trades with its required entry fields, yielding at least 40 IS and 20 OOS; at least ten vetoed and ten kept trades in each split and two observed market regimes per split are also required. Then require OOS edge retention ≥60%, reject >90% win rate or >70% OOS decay, and check stressed P&L. Those are minimum validation gates, not a guarantee of stable inference. The current journal has zero non-null Solana holder snapshots, no first-buyer/launch-block fields, and no wash transactions. Threshold P&L/PF/win rate/drawdown and stress outcomes are therefore `null`; baseline replay numbers are context only. No BSC threshold is inferred.
